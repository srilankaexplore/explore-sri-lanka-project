Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions Answered

Browse the most common questions about visiting Sri Lanka. Click any question to reveal the answer.

Most international visitors require an Electronic Travel Authorisation (ETA) to enter Sri Lanka. The ETA can be applied for online at eta.gov.lk before your arrival. It is typically valid for 30 days with the option to extend. Citizens of some South Asian countries may be exempt. Always check the current requirements with the Sri Lanka Department of Immigration before travelling.

Sri Lanka's weather patterns are governed by two monsoon seasons. The southwest monsoon (May–September) brings rain to the south and west coasts and hill country, while the northeast monsoon (October–January) affects the north and east. This means that somewhere in Sri Lanka is always sunny: December to March is ideal for the south and west coasts; April to September is perfect for the east coast. The hill country has a pleasant, cool climate year-round.

Sri Lanka is considered a very safe destination for tourists. Violent crime against tourists is rare. Petty theft can occur in crowded areas and on busy beaches, so use common sense and keep valuables secured. Sri Lanka has a welcoming, friendly culture, and locals generally go out of their way to assist visitors. Always check current travel advisories from your home country's foreign affairs department before travelling.

The currency of Sri Lanka is the Sri Lankan Rupee (LKR). ATMs are widely available in Colombo, Kandy, Galle, and all major tourist areas. Major credit and debit cards (Visa and Mastercard) are accepted at hotels, restaurants, and larger shops. It is advisable to carry some cash for tuk-tuk rides, local markets, and rural areas where card machines may not be available. Money exchange services are available at the airport and in most cities.

Sri Lanka offers several transport options. The scenic train network is highly recommended — the Colombo to Kandy and Kandy to Ella routes are among the most beautiful rail journeys in the world. Intercity buses are affordable and widespread. Tuk-tuks (three-wheelers) are the quintessential way to get around towns and short distances. For multi-day tours, hiring a private car with an experienced driver-guide is the most comfortable and flexible option. Domestic flights are also available via FitsAir.

When visiting Buddhist temples, Hindu kovils, and other religious sites, modest dress is essential. Both men and women should cover their shoulders and knees — long trousers or a sarong and a shirt or shawl are appropriate. At most popular temples, sarongs and shoulder scarves are available to borrow or hire at the entrance. You must also remove your shoes and hats before entering a place of worship. Respectful behaviour — speaking quietly and following the guidance of temple keepers — is always appreciated.

Sri Lanka rewards all durations of travel. A 5-day trip allows you to cover the Cultural Triangle (Sigiriya, Kandy, Anuradhapura) or a combination of beach and city. A 7-10 day trip enables a comprehensive circuit combining heritage, hill country, and beach. For the full experience — exploring all eight of our featured destinations in a relaxed manner — allow 10–14 days. Use our Travel Planner to build an itinerary tailored to exactly how much time you have.

It is generally not recommended to drink tap water in Sri Lanka unless it has been boiled or filtered. Bottled water is widely available and inexpensive throughout the country. Most hotels and guesthouses provide filtered or bottled water for guests. Ice in restaurants and hotels in tourist areas is typically made from purified water, but it is wise to confirm this in local eateries. Coconut water (thambili) — served fresh from the nut — is a delicious, safe, and hydrating alternative that is widely available for a very low cost.
